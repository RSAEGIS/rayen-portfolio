01 Behind the timeline

I design movement. Titles that hit, transitions that breathe, grades that give a story its temperature. Every project starts on paper, ends in a render queue — and in between it has to feel inevitable.

Six years between After Effects and DaVinci Resolve, working with YouTube channels, agencies and brands that need visuals people remember. Motion, identity, grade — one pipeline, one person, no handoff losses.

02 The journey so far

Six years, one pipeline — from a first freelance gig to a studio full of retainer clients.

  1. 2020

    First paid edit

    A 90-second product cut for a local brand — the one that proved this could be a job, not a hobby.

  2. 2021

    Moved into motion design

    Traded straight cuts for kinetic type and shape animation — After Effects became the daily driver.

  3. 2022

    Picked up color grading

    DaVinci Resolve joined the pipeline; clients started asking for the full package instead of splitting it across two people.

  4. 2023

    First agency retainer

    A monthly slot for a growing YouTube channel — steady work, tighter turnarounds, a sharper pipeline.

  5. 2024

    180+ projects shipped

    Motion, grade and brand work across agencies, creators and small studios — one pipeline, no handoff losses.

  6. 2026

    Refining the craft — and the page it lives on

    Still shipping, still tightening the pipeline, still chasing the frame that makes people stop scrolling.

15 Good to know

A 60-second motion piece usually runs 5–8 working days from locked brief to master. Long-form edits and full grades depend on footage volume — you get a dated schedule before we start.

Footage or a script, brand assets (logo in vector if you have it), references you like, and the delivery formats. If the logo only exists as a PNG I redraw it in Illustrator first.

Two full rounds are included at every stage — direction, animation, grade. Anything beyond that is billed by the hour, and I always tell you before it gets there.

23 How I work

Four things that stay true no matter what the brief is.

01

Story before style

Motion serves the story, not the other way around — a trick that doesn't earn its frame gets cut before delivery.

02

One pipeline, no handoffs

Motion, grade and design come from the same person, so nothing gets lost translating a brief between specialists.

03

Deadlines are a design constraint

A great frame that misses the launch date isn't a great frame — scope gets set to fit the calendar, not the reverse.

04

Feedback in plain language

No jargon, no gatekeeping the process — you always know why a shot looks the way it looks, and what changing it costs.
